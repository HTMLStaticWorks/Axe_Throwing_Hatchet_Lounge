The 3 Most Common
Axe Throwing Mistakes

By Rafe Calloway May 10, 2026

Axe throwing looks simple, right? You pick up a sharp object, chuck it at a target, and hope for the best. But if you want to actually hit the bullseye (and do it consistently), there's a lot of technique involved.

1. The "Death Grip"

One of the most common mistakes beginners make is gripping the axe too tightly. They think more power equals a better stick, but it's actually the opposite. A tight grip prevents the axe from releasing smoothly, leading to under-rotation.

Pro Tip: The Handshake

Grip the axe like you're giving a firm handshake. Your fingers should be wrapped around the handle, but you shouldn't be squeezing the life out of it. Let the momentum do the work.

2. Stepping Too Early

Your power comes from your legs, but timing is everything. Many throwers step forward before their arm has reached the peak of the throw. This disconnects your upper and lower body power, resulting in a weak throw that bounces off the target.

Beginner Note

If you're just starting, try throwing from a stationary stance first. Once you've mastered the arm motion, then incorporate the step to add power and distance.

3. Flicking the Wrist

This is the hardest habit to break. Most people want to flick their wrist at the end of the throw to get more spin. In axe throwing, the spin should come from the natural release of the handle, not a flick. Flicking creates unpredictable rotation and usually ends with a flat landing.

Safety Tip

Never try to catch an axe that bounces back. Even if you think you can grab the handle, the risk isn't worth it. Step back and let it fall.

Mastering these three fundamentals will take you from a casual thrower to a league-ready competitor in no time. Ready to put these tips to the test? Signup this weekend and show us what you've got!
